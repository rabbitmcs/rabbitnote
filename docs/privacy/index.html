Privacy policy

Last updated 5 November 2026

RabbitNote has no user accounts or servers of its own, and no analytics or tracking. We never receive your notes, images or passphrase.

What stays on your computer

Your notes, images, settings and Google sign-in token are stored on your computer. Notes and images are encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key made from your passphrase, and your sign-in token is encrypted with that key too. Your passphrase is never stored or sent anywhere. If you forget it, nobody can recover your notes.

Google Drive sync

Sync is optional. If you turn it on, RabbitNote asks Google for one permission: to see, edit, create and delete only the Google Drive files you use with the app. It can't see the rest of your Drive.

RabbitNote uses this permission only to keep your own encrypted notebook files in a RabbitNote folder in your Drive. They go directly between your computer and Google. We don't receive them or your Google data. Google can't read the contents of these files, but it can see details such as your account, file sizes and when files change. Google's own privacy policy applies to your Google account.

RabbitNote's use of information received from Google APIs follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Your Google data is not sold, shared with anyone, used for advertising, or used to train AI models.

Google Fonts

If you choose a Google font, RabbitNote downloads it from Google once and keeps a copy. As with any download, Google receives your computer's IP address. The built-in fonts make no such request.

Removing your data

  • To stop sync on one computer, choose Disconnect in Settings.
  • To remove RabbitNote's access, open your Google Account connections.
  • To delete synced copies, delete the RabbitNote folder from your Google Drive.
  • To delete local copies, uninstall RabbitNote and delete its data folder from your computer.

This website

These pages use no cookies of their own. Our web server may keep standard logs, such as IP addresses and pages requested, to keep the site running.
